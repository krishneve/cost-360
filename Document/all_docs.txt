1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the functional and non-functional requirements, system architecture, database design, business rules, and validation logic for Cost360, a manufacturing cost intelligence, product costing and audit platform. The document translates the macro-level functional requirement note supplied for the project into an implementation-oriented specification suitable for design, development, database construction, UI implementation, testing and academic evaluation.
This document is written primarily for a student development team implementing Cost360 using PHP, HTML5/CSS3/JavaScript and PostgreSQL as a final-year engineering project, and secondarily as a reference for project evaluators.
1.2 Scope
Cost360 is a multi-tenant, web-based platform that ingests manufacturing cost data (trial balance, inventory, production, BOM and master data) from customer-supplied source files, maps and validates that data into a canonical cost data model, computes inventory valuation, BOM cost roll-up, product costing, overhead allocation and variance analysis, reconciles results against source financial data, and presents the results through role-specific dashboards, reports, an audit desk, and an AI-assisted analytics layer.
In scope for this SRS:
Multi-tenant customer/company/division/plant/warehouse hierarchy and administration
Upload Centre, source data management, mapping (manual and AI-assisted), and data validation/quality scoring
Inventory valuation (FIFO, Periodic Weighted Average, Moving Weighted Average, Specific Cost)
BOM management, BOM explosion and BOM cost roll-up
Standard costing, product costing, overhead allocation, standard-vs-actual and variance analysis
Reconciliation (trial balance, inventory, production, costing), Audit Desk, period close and costing versioning
Management, CMA and Product dashboards; reporting; search and drill-down; audit trail and data lineage
An AI layer providing AI-assisted mapping, a CMA AI Copilot, natural-language analytics and a Costing Integrity Score, subject to AI governance controls
Explicitly out of scope for the MVP (see Section 36, Part 4, for the full MVP/Phase split): live ERP API integrations, WIP/scrap/co-product costing, cost simulation/what-if modelling, make-or-buy analysis, capacity analytics, and predictive/forecasting AI — these are Phase 2/Phase 3 roadmap items.

1.4 Product Overview
Cost360 is positioned as “Manufacturing Cost Intelligence, Product Costing & Audit Platform” with the tagline “Think Beyond Cost.” It is designed around a canonical manufacturing cost data model rather than being hard-coded to any single customer's Excel layout, so that new source formats can be onboarded through mapping configuration rather than code changes. The Cost & Management Accountant (CMA) remains the reviewer, controller and decision-maker throughout; automation (including AI) assists with repetitive processing, validation, reconciliation and exception identification, but never silently alters financial or costing results (see Section 43 principles, carried through Sections 21, 23 and 43 of the full document).
1.5 Business Problem
Manufacturing organisations typically compute product cost using fragmented spreadsheets, inconsistent valuation methods across plants, and manual reconciliation between the trial balance, inventory records and production data. This makes product costing slow, error-prone, difficult to audit, and difficult to compare across plants, periods or product lines. Cost360 addresses this by standardising the intake, mapping, validation, calculation and audit of manufacturing cost data across companies, divisions, plants and warehouses within a single governed platform.
1.6 Product Vision
To be the system of record for how a manufacturing enterprise arrives at, defends and improves its product cost — every number traceable from the dashboard back to the original source row, every calculation reproducible, and every material change audited.
1.7 Objectives
Provide a standard, auditable pipeline from raw source data to costing results, independent of source file format
Support multiple inventory valuation methods and BOM-based product costing with full cost roll-up
Provide standard-vs-actual and variance analysis with drill-down to root cause
Reconcile costing results against trial balance and inventory records before results are finalised
Provide role-specific dashboards and a governed audit desk with period close and version control
Introduce AI assistance for mapping and analytics without compromising auditability or control
Deliver the platform on a PHP + PostgreSQL technology stack suitable for academic implementation while following enterprise-grade architectural principles

2. Overall Description
2.1 Product Perspective
Cost360 is a new, self-contained, multi-tenant SaaS product. It is not a module of an existing ERP, though it is designed to ingest data exported from ERPs/accounting systems (as flat files, in the MVP) and, in future phases, to connect to them via API (Section 22, Part 3). Cost360 sits downstream of a customer's transactional systems (ERP, MES, spreadsheets) and upstream of management decision-making: it consumes exported data and produces costing results, analytics and audit evidence.
2.2 Product Functions (Summary)
Tenant, organisation hierarchy and user/role administration
Data intake via Upload Centre, with mapping (manual and AI-assisted) and validation/data-quality scoring
Master data management (items, BOM, standard cost, UOM, cost centres, rates, etc.) with governance
Inventory valuation, BOM explosion and cost roll-up, product costing and overhead allocation
Standard-vs-actual, variance, yield and cost bridge analysis
Reconciliation of costing results to trial balance, inventory and production records
Audit Desk, exception management, period close, costing versioning, audit trail and data lineage
Management, CMA and Product dashboards; a report catalogue with drill-down; search
AI layer: AI-assisted mapping, CMA AI Copilot, natural-language analytics, Costing Integrity Score, under AI governance
2.3 User Characteristics
Users range from technically supervised Platform Administrators (Cost360's own operations staff) to domain-expert Cost Accountants/CMAs (the primary power users, expected to have strong cost-accounting knowledge but not necessarily strong technical/IT knowledge) to Customer Administrators (customer-side IT/admin staff managing their own tenant configuration) to Management users (read-mostly, dashboard/report consumers) to Auditors/Reviewers (read-and-annotate users focused on evidence and sign-off). See Section 4 for detailed role profiles.

2.9 System Boundaries
Cost360 owns everything from the point a source file is uploaded (or, in future, an API payload is received) through mapping, validation, calculation, reconciliation, audit and reporting. Cost360 does not own or modify the customer's source ERP/accounting system; it treats uploaded/ingested data as read-only evidence and never writes back to source systems in the MVP (see Data Lineage principles, Section 30, Part 3).

4.2 Platform Administrator
The Platform Administrator is Cost360's own operations role (not a customer employee). Responsibilities: onboarding new customer tenants; configuring subscription/plan parameters; managing platform-wide configuration (e.g. AI provider settings, global lookups); monitoring system health, background jobs and logs; providing cross-tenant support without altering a tenant's financial results directly. This role does not participate in a tenant's costing workflow and has no permission to approve or finalise a tenant's costing results — Proposed / To Be Confirmed whether emergency “break-glass” access is permitted, and if so, under what audit controls.
4.3 CMA / Cost Accountant
The CMA is the primary operating role and the intended “brain” of the system: uploading and mapping source data, reviewing data-quality and validation results, running the costing engines, investigating variances and exceptions, performing reconciliation, and approving/finalising/locking costing periods. The CMA Dashboard (Section 13, Part 3) is built specifically around this role's day-to-day workflow: upload status, mapping status, data quality, reconciliation status, costing status, exceptions, period-close status and AI observations awaiting review.
4.4 Customer Administrator
A customer-side administrative role scoped to their own tenant. Responsible for setting up the company/division/plant/warehouse hierarchy, creating and managing users within the tenant, and assigning roles. Does not perform costing operations and cannot approve or finalise financial results — this separation of duties is a deliberate control (see BR-SEC-001, Part 3).
4.5 Customer Management / Management User
A largely read-only role for business decision-makers (e.g. plant heads, finance leadership) who consume Cost360's output — dashboards, product profitability, cost trends, plant/period comparisons — without performing data entry or approvals. Access is scoped to the companies/divisions/plants the user is authorised for.
4.6 Auditor / Reviewer
An independent role focused on reviewing the integrity of the costing process rather than performing it. The Auditor operates primarily through the Audit Desk, Audit Trail and Data Lineage screens, can annotate and flag items, and performs sign-off at defined audit checkpoints (e.g. before period lock) without being able to alter source data, mappings, or costing calculations directly.

7. System Architecture
7.1 Architectural Style
Cost360 uses a modular layered architecture. Microservices are explicitly not used for the academic MVP; instead, the codebase is organised into clearly separated layers and modules within a single PHP application, so that the design principles of separation of concerns and testability are preserved without the operational overhead of a distributed system. This keeps the architecture appropriate for a final-year engineering project while remaining a defensible baseline for later decomposition (Phase 2/3) if desired.

7.3 Layer Interaction Principle
Requests flow strictly downward: Presentation → Controller → Business Logic → (Engines / Data Processing as applicable) → Database Access → PostgreSQL, with the Reporting Layer and AI Integration Layer consumed by the Controller layer as peer services. The Presentation Layer never calls the Database Access Layer directly, and the Costing/Calculation Engines never perform their own authorisation checks — access control is enforced once, at the Controller layer, per BR-SEC-003 (Part 3).
7.4 Canonical Data Model Principle
All engines operate exclusively on the Standard Data Model (the canonical schema described in the Data Dictionary, Section 16, Part 2), never on raw uploaded data directly. This is what allows Cost360 to support “any source”: the Mapping Engine is the only component responsible for translating a given customer's source layout into the standard model; everything downstream (validation, engines, reporting) is source-format-agnostic.

Description
Authenticate a registered user against their tenant and issue an authorised session.

All roles

Username/email format; password non-empty; CAPTCHA after repeated failures — Proposed.

Permissions
N/A (pre-authorisation)

Every login attempt (success and failure) recorded in login_history with timestamp, IP address, and outcome.

Valid credentials for an active user result in a session scoped to the correct tenant and role within 2 seconds (Proposed Target — To Be Confirmed); invalid credentials never authenticate.

5.2 Upload Centre and Mapping
FR-UPL-001 — Upload Source File   (Priority: Must-Have)
Description
Allow a CMA/Cost Accountant to upload a source data file (e.g. trial balance, stock statement, BOM extract, production data) for a given tenant/company/plant/period into the Upload Centre.
Actor
CMA / Cost Accountant
Preconditions
Target period is not locked (Section 12, period status ≠ Locked); actor has Upload permission.
Trigger
Actor selects a source type and file on the Upload Centre screen.

• Actor selects Company/Division/Plant/Warehouse, Period, Source Type (e.g. Trial Balance, Stock Statement, BOM, Production).

Alternate Flow
If a Source System / file layout was previously mapped and is recognised by signature (e.g. matching column headers), system pre-applies the existing Mapping Configuration and advances status directly to “Pending Validation.”
Exception Flow
• Unsupported file type → rejected with error, nothing stored as an Upload record beyond a failed-attempt log.
• File exceeds maximum size (Proposed Target — To Be Confirmed) → rejected with error.

Business Rules
BR-UPL-001 (uploaded file is immutable once stored), BR-PER-001 (locked periods cannot be silently modified).
Validation Rules
File type in {xlsx, xls, csv} — Proposed; file not empty; period open; mandatory selection of company/plant/source type.
Inputs
File, Company, Division, Plant, Warehouse, Period, Source Type.
Outputs
Upload record; stored original file; queued mapping task.
DB entities
uploads, upload_files, source_systems, periods
Permissions
U on Upload Centre
Audit
Audit log + Data Lineage root node created: upload ID, file hash, actor, timestamp — this becomes the origin point for all downstream lineage (Section 30, Part 3).
Acceptance Criteria
A valid file for an open period is stored immutably, an Upload record is created, and the file is queued for mapping; an invalid file is rejected with a specific, actionable error.
Source
Upload Centre / Raw source data (source functional note).
FR-MAP-001 — Configure and Apply Field Mapping   (Priority: Must-Have)
Description
Map columns/fields of an uploaded source file onto the corresponding fields of the canonical Standard Data Model, either by reusing a saved Mapping Configuration or by defining a new one.
Actor
CMA / Cost Accountant
Preconditions
Upload exists with status “Pending Mapping.”
Trigger
Actor opens the upload in Mapping Configuration.
Main Flow
• System displays source columns alongside canonical target fields for the selected Source Type.
• Actor maps each source column to a target field (or marks it Ignored), optionally applying a transformation (e.g. date format, UOM conversion reference).
• Actor saves the mapping as a reusable Mapping Configuration tied to the Source System.
• System applies the mapping to transform raw rows into Standard Data Model records and sets Upload status to “Pending Validation.”
Alternate Flow
AI-Assisted Mapping (FR-MAP-002) pre-fills suggested mappings for actor confirmation before save.
Exception Flow
• A mandatory target field has no source mapping → mapping cannot be saved as “Complete”; Upload remains “Pending Mapping” with a flagged gap.
• Transformation failure on a subset of rows (e.g. unparseable date) → those rows are flagged for the Validation stage rather than silently dropped.
Business Rules
BR-MAP-001 (a mandatory canonical field must be mapped or explicitly marked Not Applicable with reason), BR-MAP-002 (mapping changes to an already-processed upload require re-validation).
Validation Rules
All mandatory canonical fields mapped or justified; no duplicate target mapping from two source columns without an aggregation rule.
Inputs
Source columns, target canonical fields, transformation rules.
Outputs
Mapping Configuration record; Standard Data Model records (pre-validation).
DB entities
mapping_configurations, mapping_dictionary, standard_data_records (or per-domain staging tables), uploads
Permissions
C, E, Run on Mapping Configuration
Audit
Audit log: mapping saved/changed, actor, fields changed, timestamp; lineage link from each Standard Data Model record back to its source row.
Acceptance Criteria
A saved mapping transforms 100% of source rows into Standard Data Model records or explicitly flags rows that cannot be transformed, with no silent data loss.
Source
Mapping / Raw source data → Standard Data Model (source functional note pipeline).
FR-MAP-002 — AI-Assisted Mapping Suggestion   (Priority: Should-Have)
Description
Use the AI Integration Layer to suggest column-to-field mappings based on column names, sample values and prior mappings for similar source layouts, to accelerate FR-MAP-001.
Actor
CMA / Cost Accountant (consumer); AI Layer (producer of suggestions)

• System pre-fills the mapping screen with suggestions, clearly marked as AI-suggested and unconfirmed.
• Actor reviews, edits and confirms each suggestion before save (per FR-MAP-001).
Alternate Flow
Low-confidence suggestions (below a Proposed threshold, To Be Confirmed) are shown but not pre-selected, requiring explicit actor action.
Exception Flow
AI service unavailable → mapping screen falls back to fully manual mapping (FR-MAP-001) with no suggestions; failure is logged, not surfaced as a blocking error to the actor.
Business Rules
BR-AI-001 (AI suggestions are never auto-applied without human confirmation — see Section 43 principle), BR-AI-002 (every AI suggestion accepted or rejected is logged for AI governance/accuracy tracking).
Validation Rules
AI suggestions are subject to the same mandatory-field and validation rules as manual mappings once confirmed.
Inputs
Source column headers, sample values (limited rows).
Outputs
Suggested mappings with confidence scores.

ai_mapping_results, mapping_configurations

V, A, R on AI Insights (actor accepts/rejects); Cfg on AI provider settings (Platform Admin)
Audit
Each suggestion logged with confidence score, and whether accepted/rejected/edited by the actor — feeds AI Governance reporting (Section 23, Part 3).

Suggested mappings never overwrite a confirmed mapping without explicit actor action; every suggestion is traceable to whether it was accepted as-is, edited, or rejected.

5.4 Inventory Valuation
FR-INV-001 — Compute Inventory Valuation   (Priority: Must-Have)
Description
Compute period-end inventory valuation for each item/warehouse using the valuation method configured for that item/plant: FIFO, Periodic Weighted Average, Moving Weighted Average, or Specific Cost.
Actor
CMA / Cost Accountant (triggers); Inventory Valuation Engine (executes)
Preconditions
Opening balance, receipts, issues and adjustments for the period are validated (FR-VAL-001 passed) for the item/warehouse/period.
Trigger
Actor runs Inventory Valuation for a period/plant, or it runs as part of the Costing Workbench orchestration.
Main Flow
• Engine loads opening balance, receipt transactions (with cost), issue transactions (quantity), and adjustments for each item/warehouse in the period.
• Engine applies the configured valuation method (see method-specific rules below) to compute issue costs and closing balance/value.
• Engine validates BR-INV-001 (Opening + Receipts − Issues ± Adjustments = Closing) as an internal consistency check.
• Engine writes valuation results (per item/warehouse) with full layer/lot detail where applicable (FIFO/Specific Cost) for lineage.
Alternate Flow
• FIFO: consumes cost layers in receipt order; retains remaining open layers for next period.
• Periodic Weighted Average: (Opening Value + Receipts Value) / (Opening Qty + Receipts Qty) applied to all issues in the period.
• Moving Weighted Average: recomputes the average cost after every receipt transaction, applied to subsequent issues until the next receipt.
• Specific Cost: each issue is costed at the specific lot/serial cost identified on the transaction — requires lot-level receipt tracking.
Exception Flow
• Negative inventory (issues exceed available quantity) → flagged as an Exception per BR-INV-002 — Proposed handling: block valuation for that item pending correction, To Be Confirmed vs. allow with warning.
• Missing cost on a receipt → Error-severity validation finding (should have been caught at FR-VAL-001; engine re-checks defensively).
Business Rules
BR-INV-001, BR-INV-002 (negative inventory handling), BR-UOM-001.
Validation Rules
Item/warehouse valuation method is configured; all transactions for the period are validated; UOM consistent or convertible.
Inputs
Opening balances, inventory transactions (receipts/issues/adjustments), item valuation method configuration.
Outputs
Inventory valuation results per item/warehouse/period; cost layers (FIFO/Specific Cost); feeds Product Costing (FR-COST-001).
DB entities
inventory_transactions, inventory_valuation_results, inventory_cost_layers, items
Permissions
Run, A on Inventory Valuation
Audit
Full lineage from valuation result back to each contributing transaction and its source upload row (Section 30, Part 3).
Acceptance Criteria
For a given method, the computed closing value satisfies BR-INV-001 exactly (to configured decimal precision) and reproduces identically on re-run against unchanged inputs (reproducibility principle, Section 43).
Source
Inventory / Inventory valuation / FIFO / Periodic Weighted Average / Moving Weighted Average / Specific Cost (source functional note).
5.5 BOM, Explosion and Cost Roll-up
FR-BOM-001 — BOM Explosion and Cost Roll-up   (Priority: Must-Have)
Description
Explode a multi-level Bill of Materials for a finished/semi-finished product into its full component tree and roll up cost from the lowest-level component upward to compute the total standard/actual cost of the parent item.
Actor
CMA / Cost Accountant (triggers); BOM Engine (executes)
Preconditions
BOM Master exists and is active for the item/period; component items have a valid cost source (standard cost, or valuation result from FR-INV-001).
Trigger
Actor runs BOM Explosion / Cost Roll-up, or it runs as part of Product Costing (FR-COST-001).
Main Flow
• Engine loads the BOM header and all component lines recursively (multi-level).
• Engine detects and rejects circular BOM structures before proceeding (BR-BOM-001).
• Engine explodes the tree to the lowest-level (purchased/raw) components, applying UOM conversion and quantity-per rules at each level.
• Engine rolls up cost bottom-up: each level's cost = sum of (component cost × quantity-per, adjusted for scrap/yield where configured) plus that level's own conversion cost (labour/machine/overhead where applicable).
• Engine writes the exploded tree and rolled-up cost to BOM Cost Roll-up results, feeding Product Costing (FR-COST-001).
Alternate Flow
Phantom BOM levels (pass-through, no separate costing) are exploded through without adding a costing layer — Proposed / To Be Confirmed if phantom BOM is in MVP scope.
Exception Flow
• Circular reference detected → explosion blocked with error identifying the cycle (BR-BOM-001).
• Missing cost for a leaf component → roll-up blocked for the affected branch; exception raised to Cost Investigation (FR-COST-004 area).
Business Rules
BR-BOM-001 (circular BOM must be detected), BR-BOM-002 (BOM version must be effective-dated and only one active version per item/period — Proposed), BR-UOM-001.
Validation Rules
BOM component quantities > 0; UOM convertible to parent UOM; no self-reference at any level.
Inputs
BOM Master (header + components, multi-level), item cost sources.
Outputs
Exploded BOM tree; rolled-up standard/actual cost per item.
DB entities
bom_header, bom_components, standard_costs, items, cost_components
Permissions
C, E, Run, A on BOM / Cost Roll-up
Audit
Lineage from rolled-up cost down through every BOM level to each component's cost source.
Acceptance Criteria
A circular BOM is always rejected before roll-up; roll-up cost equals the sum of correctly UOM-converted, quantity-weighted component costs plus this level's conversion cost, reproducibly.
Source
BOM / BOM explosion / BOM cost roll-up (source functional note).
5.6 Product Costing, Overhead Allocation and Variance
FR-COST-001 — Compute Product Cost   (Priority: Must-Have)
Description
Compute the total standard and/or actual cost of a finished product for a period by combining material cost (from BOM roll-up), labour cost, machine/process cost, allocated overheads, and other direct costs.
Actor
CMA / Cost Accountant (triggers); Cost Engine (executes)
Preconditions
BOM cost roll-up (FR-BOM-001) and Inventory Valuation (FR-INV-001) are complete for the period; Overhead Allocation (FR-COST-002) rates are configured.
Trigger
Actor runs Product Costing for a period/plant/product (or product set), typically from the Costing Workbench.
Main Flow
• Engine retrieves material cost from BOM Cost Roll-up.
• Engine retrieves/computes labour cost using Labour Rate Master and recorded labour hours/quantities.
• Engine retrieves/computes machine/process cost using Machine Rate Master and recorded machine hours/quantities.
• Engine retrieves allocated overheads from the Overhead Allocation run (FR-COST-002).
• Engine computes Product Cost = Material + Labour + Process/Machine + Production Overheads + Other Direct Cost + Allocated Overheads (BR-COST-001).
• Engine writes the Product Costing result, tagged with a Costing Version (Section 12/21) for audit and comparison.
Alternate Flow
Costing can be run in Standard mode (uses Standard Cost Master rates) or Actual mode (uses period-actual rates/quantities) — both feed Standard vs Actual comparison (FR-COST-003).
Exception Flow
Any missing cost component (e.g. overhead not yet allocated) blocks finalisation of the Product Costing result for that product, surfaced as an exception.
Business Rules
BR-COST-001 (product cost formula), BR-PER-001 (locked periods).
Validation Rules
All contributing components (material, labour, machine, overhead) must be present and validated before the result can be Finalised (vs. left as Draft/Provisional).
Inputs
BOM roll-up cost, labour data, machine data, overhead allocation results, cost component classification.
Outputs
Product Costing result per product/plant/period/version; feeds dashboards, reports, variance analysis.
DB entities
costing_runs, costing_results, cost_components, labour_rates, machine_rates, overhead_allocations
Permissions
Run, A, F on Product Costing
Audit
Costing run logged with inputs snapshot (or references to versioned inputs) sufficient to reproduce the result exactly — reproducibility principle (Section 43).
Acceptance Criteria
Given identical, unchanged inputs, re-running Product Costing produces an identical result; the result's cost breakdown reconciles component-by-component to BR-COST-001.
Source
Product Costing / Standard Cost (source functional note).
FR-COST-002 — Overhead Allocation   (Priority: Must-Have)
Description
Allocate indirect/overhead costs (e.g. factory overhead, cost-centre expenses) to products or cost objects using a configured allocation basis (e.g. machine hours, labour hours, material value).
Actor
CMA / Cost Accountant (triggers/configures); Allocation Engine (executes)
Preconditions
Allocation Rule Master is configured for the cost pool and period; the allocation base (e.g. total machine hours) is available from validated data.
Trigger
Actor runs Overhead Allocation for a period/plant, typically before Product Costing (FR-COST-001).
Main Flow
• Engine identifies overhead cost pools for the period from the Trial Balance / GL Classification.
• Engine reads the configured Allocation Rule (basis and driver) per cost pool.
• Engine computes the allocation rate = cost pool amount / total driver quantity.
• Engine allocates cost to each product/cost object = allocation rate × that object's driver quantity.
• Engine writes Overhead Allocation results, which feed Product Costing (FR-COST-001).
Alternate Flow
Multiple allocation bases can be configured for different cost pools within the same period (e.g. machine-hour basis for factory overhead, labour-hour basis for supervision cost).
Exception Flow
Total driver quantity is zero for a cost pool → allocation cannot be computed; raised as an exception requiring CMA resolution (e.g. reconfigure basis).
Business Rules
BR-COST-001 (feeds product cost), BR-ALLOC-001 (sum of allocated amounts must equal the cost pool amount, within rounding tolerance) — Proposed rule, To Be Confirmed tolerance.
Validation Rules
Allocation Rule Master fully configured for every overhead cost pool before the run is allowed to Finalise.
Inputs
Overhead cost pools (from TB/GL), Allocation Rule Master, driver quantities.
Outputs
Overhead Allocation results per cost object/period.
DB entities
allocation_rules, overhead_allocations, cost_centres, gl_classification
Permissions
C, E, Run, A on Overhead Allocation
Audit
Allocation run logged with rule version, cost pool amounts, driver quantities and computed rates for full traceability.
Acceptance Criteria
Sum of amounts allocated from a cost pool equals the cost pool's total amount (BR-ALLOC-001); every allocated product cost line is traceable to its driving rule and driver quantity.
Source
Overhead Allocation (source functional note).
FR-COST-003 — Standard vs Actual and Variance Analysis   (Priority: Must-Have)
Description
Compare Standard-mode and Actual-mode Product Costing results for the same product/period and decompose the difference into named variance categories (material price/usage, labour rate/efficiency, overhead volume/spending, yield) with drill-down to root cause.
Actor
CMA / Cost Accountant
Preconditions
Both Standard and Actual Product Costing results exist for the product/period (FR-COST-001 run in both modes).
Trigger
Actor opens Standard vs Actual / Variance Analysis for a product/period.
Main Flow
• System retrieves Standard and Actual costing results for the selected product/period.
• System computes total variance = Actual Cost − Standard Cost.
• System decomposes total variance into material (price and usage), labour (rate and efficiency), and overhead (volume and spending) sub-variances using standard cost-accounting formulas.
• System presents the Cost Bridge (waterfall from Standard to Actual) and allows drill-down from any variance figure to the contributing transactions.
Alternate Flow
Yield variance is separated out where BOM yield/scrap configuration exists — see Yield Analysis (module spec, Section 8).
Exception Flow
Missing Actual data for a component (e.g. no actual labour hours recorded) → that sub-variance is flagged “Incomplete” rather than computed as zero, to avoid misleading figures.
Business Rules
BR-VAR-001 (variance formulas per category) — Proposed formula set, To Be Confirmed against source note's exact variance taxonomy.
Validation Rules
Both Standard and Actual results must belong to the same Costing Version family / comparable basis before comparison is allowed.
Inputs
Standard and Actual Product Costing results.
Outputs
Variance Analysis result; Cost Bridge; drill-down links into Cost Investigation.
DB entities
costing_results, variances, cost_bridge_segments
Permissions
V, Run, A on Standard vs Actual / Variance Analysis
Audit
Every variance figure traceable to the two costing runs it was derived from.
Acceptance Criteria
Sum of decomposed sub-variances equals the total variance (Actual − Standard) to configured rounding tolerance; every variance is drillable to source transactions.
Source
Standard vs Actual / Variance Analysis / Cost Bridge / Cost Change Investigation (source functional note).
5.7 Reconciliation
FR-REC-001 — Trial Balance and Inventory Reconciliation   (Priority: Must-Have)
Description
Reconcile costing-relevant balances (e.g. inventory value, COGS, overhead absorbed) computed by Cost360 against the customer's Trial Balance and inventory records for the same period, to confirm costing results are consistent with the books before they are finalised.

Preconditions

Production and Consumption Reconciliation follow the same pattern against production/consumption source records rather than the GL.

Inputs
Trial Balance, computed inventory/costing results.
Outputs
Reconciliation result and exception list per period/plant.
DB entities
reconciliations, reconciliation_exceptions, trial_balance, inventory_valuation_results, costing_results
Permissions
Run, A on Reconciliation
Audit
Every reconciliation run and every exception resolution (including accepted-reason overrides) is logged with actor and justification.
Acceptance Criteria
A period cannot be locked (FR-AUD-002) while unresolved reconciliation exceptions beyond tolerance remain open.
Source
Reconciliation / TB Reconciliation / Inventory Reconciliation / Production Reconciliation / Consumption Reconciliation (source functional note).
5.8 Audit Desk and Period Close
FR-AUD-001 — Audit Desk Exception Review and Sign-off   (Priority: Must-Have)
Description
Provide a consolidated Audit Desk where all open exceptions (validation errors overridden, reconciliation exceptions, costing exceptions, AI-flagged observations) across a period/plant are reviewed, annotated, and approved or rejected by an Auditor/Reviewer or authorised CMA.
Actor
Auditor / Reviewer, CMA / Cost Accountant
Preconditions
One or more exceptions exist for the period/plant.
Trigger
Actor opens the Audit Desk for a period/plant.
Main Flow
• System lists all open exceptions with source module, severity, description and current status.
• Actor opens an exception, reviews evidence (via Data Lineage drill-down), and adds an annotation/resolution note.
• Actor approves (accepts the exception with justification) or rejects (sends back for correction) the exception.
• System updates exception status and records the decision in the Audit Trail.
Alternate Flow
Exceptions can be bulk-triaged by severity/module — Proposed / To Be Confirmed for MVP.
Exception Flow
An exception left unresolved blocks Period Close (FR-AUD-002) for the affected scope.
Business Rules
BR-AUD-001 (every exception must have a logged resolution before period lock), BR-SEC-001 (separation of duties between the actor who caused an exception and the actor who approves it, where practical) — Proposed, To Be Confirmed.
Validation Rules
A resolution note is mandatory when approving/rejecting an exception.
Inputs
Exception list, actor annotations and decisions.
Outputs
Updated exception status; Audit Trail entries.
DB entities
exceptions, audit_trail, data_lineage
Permissions
V, E, A, R, Aud on Audit Desk
Audit
Every review action (open, annotate, approve, reject) logged with old/new status, actor, timestamp, and justification text.
Acceptance Criteria
No period can be locked while it has exceptions without a logged resolution; every resolution is attributable to a specific authorised actor.
Source
Audit Desk / Exception Management (source functional note).
FR-AUD-002 — Period Close, Lock and Controlled Reopen   (Priority: Must-Have)
Description
Lock a costing period once all upstream steps (validation, costing, reconciliation, audit sign-off) are complete, preventing further silent changes; allow a controlled, audited reopen when genuinely required.
Actor
CMA / Cost Accountant (lock/reopen request); Auditor / Reviewer (sign-off, where configured)
Preconditions
For Lock: no open validation Errors, no open reconciliation exceptions beyond tolerance, no open Audit Desk exceptions for the period/plant.
Trigger
Actor initiates Period Close from the Period Close screen.
Main Flow
• System checks all lock preconditions (validation, reconciliation, audit) and displays a readiness checklist.
• If all preconditions pass, actor confirms Lock.
• System marks the period Locked, creates a new immutable Costing Version snapshot (Section 12/21), and disables further data entry/mapping/costing runs for that period/scope.
• System notifies relevant stakeholders (Management, Auditor) that the period is closed.
Alternate Flow
Reopen: an authorised CMA (or higher) requests Reopen with a mandatory reason; system requires a second-level approval (Proposed — To Be Confirmed which role approves) before unlocking; reopening creates a new Costing Version rather than overwriting the locked one.
Exception Flow
Lock attempted with unresolved preconditions → blocked, with the specific blocking items listed.
Business Rules
BR-PER-001 (locked periods cannot be silently modified), BR-PER-002 (reopen requires reason + approval and produces a new version, never overwrites history).
Validation Rules
All lock preconditions must independently pass; reopen reason is mandatory and minimum length — Proposed.
Inputs
Period identifier, scope (plant/company), lock/reopen action, reason (for reopen).
Outputs
Period status change; new Costing Version snapshot; notifications.
DB entities
periods, costing_versions, audit_trail, notifications
Permissions
Run, L, Ro on Period Close (CMA); A on reopen approval (Auditor/senior role — Proposed)
Audit
Every lock and reopen event logged with actor, timestamp, reason (for reopen), and resulting version ID; this is the anchor event for costing reproducibility (Section 43).
Acceptance Criteria
A period cannot be locked with any open blocking exception; every reopen is reasoned, approved, and produces a new traceable version rather than erasing the prior one.
Source
Period Close / Costing Versioning / Audit Trail (source functional note); Financial Integrity principles (Section 43).

5.10 AI Layer
FR-AI-001 — CMA AI Copilot — Natural-Language Analytics Query   (Priority: Should-Have)
Description
Allow a CMA to ask a natural-language question about costing data (e.g. “why did Product X's cost increase this month?”) and receive an AI-generated response that clearly separates Fact, Calculation, Inference and Prediction, with evidence links and a confidence indicator.
Actor
CMA / Cost Accountant
Preconditions
AI service is available; relevant costing data for the query scope exists.
Trigger
Actor types a question into the AI Copilot panel.
Main Flow
• System resolves the query's scope (product/period/plant) from the question and/or current screen context.
• System retrieves relevant Facts (actual data points) and Calculations (existing costing/variance results) from the database.
• System sends the question plus retrieved facts/calculations to the AI Integration Layer, which returns a structured response distinguishing Fact, Calculation, Inference and Prediction, each with a confidence indicator.
• System displays the response with citations/evidence links back to the source records (lineage), and logs the interaction.
Alternate Flow
Actor can request the underlying evidence for any Inference/Prediction statement, which opens the relevant Data Lineage / Cost Investigation view.
Exception Flow
AI service unavailable or low-confidence response → system clearly states it cannot answer reliably rather than presenting a guess as fact.
Business Rules
BR-AI-001 (AI never silently alters financial results — Copilot is read/advisory only), BR-AI-003 (every Inference/Prediction must be visually distinguished from Fact/Calculation).
Validation Rules
Response schema must include the Fact/Calculation/Inference/Prediction classification and a confidence value for non-Fact statements before being displayed.
Inputs
Natural-language question; scoped costing data.
Outputs
Structured AI response with classification, confidence, and evidence links.
DB entities
ai_observations, audit_trail
Permissions
V, A, R on AI Insights (CMA can accept/dismiss an AI observation)
Audit
Every query, response, and whether the CMA accepted/dismissed/acted on it is logged — AI Governance record (Section 23, Part 3).
Acceptance Criteria
No AI response alters a costing result directly; every non-Fact statement is labelled and carries a confidence indicator; every response is traceable to the data it drew on.
Source
AI Layer / CMA AI Copilot / Natural Language Analytics / AI Governance / Costing Integrity Score (source functional note); Section 43 Financial Integrity Rule.

15. Database Requirements
Cost360's database must support: (a) strict referential integrity across a multi-tenant hierarchy; (b) effective-dated master data so historical costing runs remain reproducible even after masters change; (c) an append-only audit trail; (d) efficient querying of large transactional volumes (inventory, production, GL) scoped by tenant/period; and (e) transactional (ACID) guarantees around any multi-step costing or reconciliation run, so a partially-completed run never leaves inconsistent results visible.
Referential integrity enforced via foreign keys at the database level, not only in application code.
All monetary values stored as NUMERIC (never FLOAT/DOUBLE) to avoid rounding drift in financial calculations.
All timestamps stored as TIMESTAMPTZ; all business dates as DATE.
Tenant isolation enforced by a mandatory tenant_id (directly or via parent) on every tenant-scoped table, validated at the Data Access Layer — see Section 17.12.
Financial/transactional tables are insert-mostly; corrections are modelled as new linked transactions, never destructive UPDATE/DELETE, once a row belongs to a locked period (Section 43).
Target data volumes: Proposed / To Be Confirmed against the academic deployment's sample dataset; design should not preclude scaling to multi-year, multi-plant transaction volumes (see Section 25, Part 3).

17. PostgreSQL Database Implementation Specification
17.1 Database Architecture
Single PostgreSQL database for the academic deployment, with tenant isolation enforced at the row level (shared schema, tenant_id discriminator) rather than schema-per-tenant or database-per-tenant — Proposed / To Be Confirmed; the row-level approach is recommended for the MVP as it is simplest to implement correctly in PHP without an ORM's automatic tenant-scoping.
17.2 Schema Strategy
A single schema (public, or a named application schema e.g. cost360) holding all application tables. Reference/lookup tables (currencies, uom) are tenant-agnostic; all transactional and master-data tables carry tenant_id directly or transitively via a foreign key to a tenant-scoped parent.
17.3 Naming Conventions
Tables: lower_snake_case, plural (e.g. bom_components).
Primary keys: <table_singular>_id, e.g. item_id, bom_id.
Foreign keys: named after the referenced entity, e.g. item_id, tenant_id.
Boolean columns: is_/has_ prefix (e.g. is_phantom).
Timestamps: _at suffix (created_at); actor references: _by suffix (created_by).
17.4 Primary Key Strategy
BIGSERIAL (or GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITY — Proposed, either acceptable) surrogate integer primary keys throughout, for join performance and simplicity in PHP application code. Natural/business keys (item_code, tenant_code) are enforced via separate UNIQUE constraints rather than used as primary keys.
17.5 Foreign Key Strategy
All foreign keys declared at the database level with ON DELETE RESTRICT by default (financial/master data must not be silently orphaned or cascaded away); ON DELETE CASCADE used only for genuinely dependent child rows with no independent audit value (e.g. bom_components under bom_header) — Proposed, to be confirmed per table during implementation.
17.6 Indexing Strategy
Every foreign key column indexed.
Composite index on (tenant_id, period_id) for all high-volume transactional tables (inventory_transactions, costing_results, etc.).
Unique indexes backing all natural-key UNIQUE constraints (item_code per tenant, etc.).
Partial indexes on status columns where a query pattern filters heavily on one status (e.g. WHERE status = 'open' on periods/exceptions) — Proposed, to be tuned against real query patterns.
17.7 Unique Constraints
Composite uniqueness (tenant_id, natural_key) is used wherever a natural key must only be unique within a tenant (e.g. item_code), rather than a global UNIQUE constraint, since multiple tenants may legitimately reuse the same code.
17.8 Check Constraints
Quantities and rates: CHECK (quantity > 0), CHECK (standard_rate >= 0), etc.
Enumerated text columns (status, txn_type, severity, etc.): CHECK (col IN (...)) rather than free text, until/unless promoted to a lookup table.
Date ranges: CHECK (effective_to IS NULL OR effective_to >= effective_from).
17.9 Audit Columns
Every table other than pure log/audit tables carries created_at, created_by, and, where the row is mutable, updated_at/updated_by. audit_trail itself is append-only (no updated_at) by design.
17.10 Effective Dating
Tables identified in the Data Dictionary as effective-dated (BOM, Standard Cost, UOM Conversion, Labour/Machine Rate, GL Classification) use an effective_from/effective_to pair rather than in-place overwrite, with a CHECK/trigger — Proposed — preventing overlapping ranges for the same natural key.
17.11 Versioning
costing_versions holds one immutable row per Period Close lock/reopen event; costing_runs, costing_results and standard_costs reference the version under which they were produced/locked, so any historical figure can be reproduced exactly (Section 43).
17.12 Tenant Isolation
Every query issued by the Database Access Layer includes an explicit tenant_id predicate sourced from the authenticated session, never from client-supplied input — this is enforced centrally in the Data Access Layer helper functions (Section 17.14) rather than left to individual query authors, to prevent accidental cross-tenant data leakage (see Security Requirements, Section 20, Part 3).
17.13 Transaction Management
Any multi-step write (e.g. a costing run writing multiple costing_results rows and updating costing_runs.status) is wrapped in a single PostgreSQL transaction so it is atomic: pg_query_params("BEGIN") / commit / rollback via the PHP Database Access Layer (Section 17.14).
17.14 Referential Integrity and PHP Database Connectivity
Per the project's technology constraint, database connectivity uses PostgreSQL's native PHP extension (pg_* functions) exclusively — PDO is not used anywhere in this design. The Database Access Layer exposes a small set of helper functions that all application code goes through, so that tenant scoping, parameterisation and transaction handling are applied consistently rather than left to each caller.
Connection management (Proposed pattern)
function db_connect(): resource {
    static $conn = null;
    if ($conn === null) {
        $conn = pg_connect(getenv('PG_DSN'));
        if (!$conn) { throw new RuntimeException('DB connection failed'); }
    }
    return $conn;
}
Parameterised query execution
function db_query(string $sql, array $params = []): array {
    $conn = db_connect();
    $result = pg_query_params($conn, $sql, $params);
    if ($result === false) {
        error_log('Query failed: ' . pg_last_error($conn));
        throw new RuntimeException('Database query failed');
    }
    return pg_fetch_all($result) ?: [];
}
Transaction wrapper
function db_transaction(callable $work) {
    $conn = db_connect();
    pg_query($conn, 'BEGIN');
    try {
        $result = $work($conn);
        pg_query($conn, 'COMMIT');
        return $result;
    } catch (Throwable $e) {
        pg_query($conn, 'ROLLBACK');
        error_log('Transaction rolled back: ' . $e->getMessage());
        throw $e;
    }
}
Environment configuration (DSN, credentials) is read from environment variables, never hard-coded — Proposed / To Be Confirmed exact secrets-management approach for the academic deployment. Query logging and error logging write to the application log (Section 24, Part 3) with query text and parameter count (not parameter values, to avoid logging sensitive data) on failure. Connection pooling is not required for the single-server academic MVP; PgBouncer is noted as a Phase 2/3 scaling option (Section 25, Part 3).

6.3 Scalability
The design should not architecturally preclude scaling to multi-year, multi-plant data volumes even though the academic deployment will run a small demonstration dataset (see Section 25 for detail). See Section 20 (Security Architecture) for the full, detailed security requirement set; this NFR section states the summary commitment: authenticated access only, RBAC-enforced authorisation, tenant data isolation, and protection against the standard OWASP web application risk classes (injection, XSS, CSRF, broken access control).
6.5 Maintainability
Achieved architecturally via the layered design (Section 7, Part 1): controllers, business logic, costing engines, data access and reporting are separated so a change in one layer (e.g. a new valuation method) does not require changes to unrelated layers (e.g. the UI).
6.6 Usability
Concrete, testable usability requirements rather than a vague “user-friendly” goal: every data-entry form validates input before submission and shows field-level errors; every long-running action (upload, costing run) shows progress and does not silently hang; every destructive-adjacent action (reopen a period, override a validation error) requires an explicit confirmation step with a stated reason.
6.7 Reliability
A failed background job (e.g. a costing run that errors mid-way) must leave the database in a consistent state (via the transaction management described in Section 17.13, Part 2) — never a partially-written costing result.
6.8 Recoverability
See Section 26 (Backup and Recovery) for detailed RPO/RTO targets (Proposed / To Be Confirmed).
6.9 Auditability
Every material create/update/approve/reject/lock/reopen action across the platform is captured in the Audit Trail (Section 21) with actor, timestamp, old/new value and, where applicable, reason — this is treated as a functional requirement of the platform, not an optional add-on, per the Financial Integrity Rule (Section 43 of the original functional note).
6.10 Data Integrity
Enforced through database constraints (Section 17.6–17.8, Part 2), engine-level self-checks (e.g. BR-INV-001, BR-COST-001), and the validation pipeline (Section 19) — defence in depth rather than reliance on a single layer.
6.11 Compatibility
Target browsers: latest two major versions of Chrome, Edge and Firefox — Proposed / To Be Confirmed. File import compatibility: xlsx, xls, csv — Proposed.
6.12 Portability
The PHP + PostgreSQL stack with no PDO dependency and no proprietary extensions is intended to run on any standard LAMP/LEMP-style Linux hosting environment without modification — Proposed design goal.
6.13 Accessibility
Proposed Target — To Be Confirmed: WCAG 2.1 AA is noted as a good-practice target for form labelling, colour contrast and keyboard navigation, but is not treated as a hard MVP gate for this academic project unless the evaluation criteria require it.

18. Business Rules
This is the consolidated catalogue of every business rule referenced throughout this SRS (Parts 1–3), gathered here as the single source of truth. “Rule” includes the trigger, the system's response when satisfied, and how violations/exceptions are handled, where applicable.
18.1 Inventory and BOM Rules
Rule ID
Module
Rule
BR-INV-001
Inventory Valuation
Opening Quantity/Value + Receipts − Issues ± Adjustments = Closing Quantity/Value, for every item/warehouse/period. Engine self-checks this identity on every valuation run (FR-INV-001); a failure blocks finalisation.
BR-INV-002
Inventory Valuation
Negative inventory (an issue exceeding available quantity) is not permitted without an explicit, logged override by an authorised CMA stating a reason — Proposed control, To Be Confirmed whether override is allowed at all in MVP.
BR-BOM-001
BOM
Circular BOM structures (an item directly or transitively containing itself as a component) must be detected and block BOM Explosion (FR-BOM-001).
BR-BOM-002
BOM
Only one BOM version may be Active for a given item on any given date; effective-dating windows must not overlap — Proposed rule.
BR-UOM-001
UOM
UOM conversions used in any calculation (BOM explosion, inventory valuation, reporting) must be effective-dated and the conversion effective on the transaction/calculation date must be used, not the latest conversion factor.
18.2 Costing Rules
Rule ID
Module
Rule
BR-COST-001
Product Costing
Product Cost = Material + Labour + Process/Machine + Production Overheads + Other Direct Cost + Allocated Overheads. Enforced by the Cost Engine and defensively by a database CHECK constraint on costing_results (Section 17.15, Part 2).
BR-ALLOC-001
Overhead Allocation
The sum of amounts allocated from a cost pool must equal the cost pool's total amount, within a configured rounding tolerance — Proposed tolerance, To Be Confirmed.
BR-VAR-001
Variance Analysis
The sum of decomposed sub-variances (material price/usage, labour rate/efficiency, overhead volume/spending) must equal the total variance (Actual − Standard) — Proposed formula set, To Be Confirmed against the source note's exact variance taxonomy.

Rule ID
Module
Rule
BR-REC-001
Reconciliation
A reconciliation variance beyond the configured tolerance (Proposed, To Be Confirmed) is raised as an open Reconciliation Exception and must be resolved (corrected or accepted with reason) before the period can be locked.
BR-AUD-001

Every exception (validation, reconciliation, costing, AI-flagged) must have a logged resolution — approved with justification or corrected — before the affected period can be locked.

20. Security Requirements
Security requirements are organised by concern below. Every item is a requirement on the implementation, cross-referenced to the relevant Business Rule (Section 18) or Functional Requirement (Section 5, Part 2) where one exists.
20.1 Authentication
Passwords stored using a strong one-way hash (bcrypt/argon2 — Proposed, To Be Confirmed); never stored or logged in plain text.
Login attempts are rate-limited and accounts lock temporarily after repeated failures (BR-SEC-004).
Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Proposed / To Be Confirmed for MVP — recommended at minimum for Platform Administrator accounts given their cross-tenant reach.
20.2 Authorization and RBAC
Every controller action checks the authenticated user's permission for that action against the RBAC Matrix (Section 4.7, Part 1) before executing (BR-SEC-003).
Permission checks fail closed: an unrecognised or missing permission denies access rather than defaulting to allow.
20.3 Tenant Isolation
Tenant scoping is enforced centrally in the Data Access Layer (Section 17.12, 17.14, Part 2), not left to individual query authors (BR-SEC-002).
Cross-tenant access is only possible for the Platform Administrator role, and even then is logged distinctly as a platform-level action in the Audit Trail.
20.4 Session Management
Session tokens are generated with a cryptographically secure random source, transmitted only over HTTPS, and marked HttpOnly/Secure — Proposed cookie flags.
Idle session timeout per BR-SEC-005; sessions invalidated server-side on logout (not merely cleared client-side).
20.5 Input Validation and Injection Protection
All database queries use parameterised queries via pg_query_params (Section 17.14, Part 2); string concatenation into SQL is prohibited by design convention.
All user-supplied input is validated server-side (never relying on client-side/JavaScript validation alone) against expected type, length and format.
20.6 XSS and CSRF Protection
All output rendered into HTML is escaped/encoded appropriately for its context (HTML body, attribute, JavaScript) to prevent stored/reflected XSS.
State-changing requests (POST/PUT/DELETE-equivalent) include a CSRF token validated server-side — Proposed implementation via a per-session token.
20.7 File Upload Security
Uploaded files are validated by content/structure, not only by file extension, before being processed (defence against disguised file types).
Uploaded files are stored outside the web-server-executable path, or with execution disabled for that storage location, to prevent uploaded content from ever being executed as server-side code.
A file hash (Section 16.1, uploads.file_hash) is recorded at upload time to support integrity verification later.
20.8 Encryption
Encryption in transit: HTTPS/TLS enforced for all client-server communication — Proposed, To Be Confirmed certificate arrangement for the academic deployment.
Encryption at rest: Proposed / To Be Confirmed for the academic deployment; recommended at minimum for password hashes (already one-way hashed) and any future storage of externally-sourced credentials (e.g. AI API keys, ERP connection secrets).
20.9 Audit Logging and Login History
See Section 21 for the full Audit Trail and login_history specification; every authentication event and every material data change is logged, non-optionally.
20.10 Database and Administrative Access Security
The PHP application connects to PostgreSQL using a dedicated application role with only the privileges it needs (no superuser), following least-privilege (Section 20.11).
Database credentials are held in environment configuration, not committed to source control — Proposed secrets-management approach for the academic deployment.
20.11 Least Privilege and Sensitive Data Handling
Every role's default permission set (Section 4.7, Part 1) grants the minimum access needed for that role's responsibilities; elevated access is the exception, not the default.
Sensitive data (passwords, any future PII beyond user name/email) is never included in application or query logs (Section 17.14, Part 2).

22. Integration Requirements
Cost360's MVP is intentionally file-based rather than API-integrated, per the source note's phasing. This section specifies the one integration that is in scope for the MVP (the AI service) and the integrations reserved for Phase 2/3.
22.1 AI Service Integration (MVP)
Outbound HTTPS calls from the AI Integration Layer (Section 7.2, Part 1) to an external AI provider — Proposed / To Be Confirmed provider and model.
Requests include only the minimum data needed for the task (e.g. column headers and a small sample of values for mapping suggestions, not entire datasets) — data-minimisation principle.
Timeouts and graceful degradation: if the AI service is slow or unavailable, the dependent feature (mapping suggestion, Copilot query) falls back to a manual/no-suggestion path rather than blocking the user (see FR-MAP-002 Exception Flow).
API keys/credentials for the AI service are held in environment configuration, never in source control or client-side code.
22.2 File Import Formats (MVP)
Supported inbound formats: .xlsx, .xls, .csv — Proposed, To Be Confirmed.
Supported export formats: on-screen, CSV, PDF, Excel — Proposed, To Be Confirmed for each report.
22.3 Future ERP / API Integrations (Phase 2/3)
Scheduled or on-demand pull of Trial Balance, inventory and production data from an ERP via API, replacing manual upload for onboarded customers.
Outbound webhook/API for notification delivery to external systems (e.g. email, Slack/Teams) — Phase 2.
A formal Cost360 public API (for customers to pull costing results into their own BI tools) — Phase 3, referenced in the source note's “future API architecture” item.
No specific ERP vendor or API contract is defined in the source note; this is explicitly flagged as To Be Confirmed and out of MVP scope.

25. Performance and Scalability
This section distinguishes what the source functional note requires (the categories that must be addressed) from proposed numeric technical targets (which require confirmation).
25.1 Large File Handling
Upload and mapping must handle source files with tens of thousands of rows without timing out — Proposed Target: up to 100,000 rows per file, To Be Confirmed against realistic customer file sizes.
File parsing runs as a background job for files above a configured row-count threshold, with progress tracking (Section 25.4), rather than blocking the HTTP request — Proposed threshold, TBC.
25.2 Transaction Volume
The schema and indexing strategy (Section 17.6, Part 2) are designed to remain performant into the millions of transaction rows per tenant across multiple years, even though the academic demonstration dataset will be far smaller.
Queries scoped by (tenant_id, period_id) rely on the composite index defined in Section 17.6 to avoid full-table scans as volume grows.
25.3 Multi-Plant and Concurrent-Customer Load
The tenant-isolation design (Section 17.12) allows multiple tenants' data to coexist in one database without cross-contamination, supporting concurrent customers on shared infrastructure for the MVP.
Concurrent costing runs across different tenants/plants must not block each other; concurrent runs within the same plant/period are serialised (Proposed — a lock on the period while a costing run is in progress, To Be Confirmed exact concurrency model).
25.4 Background Processing, Queues and Progress Tracking
Long-running operations (large file import, costing runs, reconciliation) execute as Background Jobs (background_jobs table, Section 16.2, Part 2) rather than synchronously within a web request.
Each job exposes status (Queued/Running/Completed/Failed) and, where feasible, a progress indicator (e.g. rows processed / total rows) on the Background Jobs screen (Section 9.11, Part 1).
Failed jobs retain their error detail and support a manual retry action.
25.5 Horizontal Scaling (Phase 2/3 Consideration)
The stateless-controller design (no server-side session state beyond the database-backed session) allows the PHP application tier to scale horizontally behind a load balancer in a future deployment.
Read-heavy workloads (dashboards, reports) are candidates for a PostgreSQL read replica in a future deployment; not required for the academic MVP.
26. Backup and Recovery
26.1 Backup Strategy
PostgreSQL logical backups (pg_dump) on a regular schedule — Proposed Target: daily, To Be Confirmed for the academic deployment.
Uploaded source files (File/Source Data Storage) are backed up alongside the database, since they are the root of Data Lineage (Section 21.2) and losing them would break evidentiary traceability even if the database itself is intact.
Backup retention: Proposed Target — To Be Confirmed (e.g. 30 days rolling for an academic deployment).

27. Test Strategy and Test Plan
27.1 Test Objectives
Verify every functional requirement in Section 5 (Part 2) behaves as specified, including its alternate and exception flows.
Verify every business rule (Section 18, Part 3) holds under the specific scenarios that exercise it, especially the financial-integrity rules (BR-INV-001, BR-COST-001, BR-PER-001).

Load/stress testing at production-scale transaction volumes (Section 25, Part 3) — out of scope for the academic evaluation dataset.
Formal penetration testing — out of scope; basic security test cases (Section 27.9-equivalent, see Section 28.10) cover OWASP-class risks at a functional level only.

27.5 Golden Reference Dataset
A Golden Reference Dataset is a small, hand-verified set of source files and their expected outputs, used both to validate the canonical data model end-to-end and as the regression baseline for every subsequent code change.
Trial Balance (a small, internally consistent TB with known debit=credit totals)
Stock statement / inventory transactions (opening balance, a handful of receipts and issues with known costs, covering all four valuation methods across different items)
BOM (a 2–3 level structure for at least one finished product, including one item deliberately excluded from any cycle to prove BR-BOM-001 does not false-positive)
Standard Cost (rates for every component of the BOM's items)
Product Master, UOM (including at least one non-trivial conversion), Cost Centre
Previous period data (to exercise period-over-period comparison and variance)
Production data (output quantities to drive Yield/Variance test cases)
For this dataset, expected inventory valuation, BOM roll-up cost, product cost, and variance figures are calculated by hand (or in a spreadsheet, independently of the application) ahead of time, so that a system run's output can be compared against a trusted, independently-derived expected result — this is the release-acceptance check before any Golden Reference Dataset-based regression is considered passing.
27.6 Costing Engine Testing — Priority Statement
Calculation accuracy takes precedence over UI sophistication throughout this project. A costing engine that produces a wrong number in a well-designed screen is a worse outcome than a correct number in a plain screen. Every costing engine (Inventory Valuation, BOM, Cost, Allocation, Variance, Reconciliation) must pass its Golden Reference Dataset comparison before any UI polish work on that engine's screens is considered a priority.
Engine
Priority Test Scenarios
Inventory Valuation
FIFO layer consumption order; Periodic WAC recomputation; Moving WAC recomputation after each receipt; Specific Cost lot matching; negative inventory handling; UOM conversion mid-calculation; inventory adjustment; effective-date boundary at period start/end.
BOM
Multi-level explosion (3+ levels); circular reference detection; scrap % application; UOM conversion at each level.
Costing
BR-COST-001 identity; Standard vs Actual mode switch; missing-component blocking.
Allocation
BR-ALLOC-001 identity (sum of allocations = pool amount); zero-driver edge case.
Variance
BR-VAR-001 identity (sum of sub-variances = total variance); missing-actual-data flagging.
Reconciliation
Tolerance boundary (exactly at, just inside, just outside); multiple recon types for the same period.

38. Glossary
Term
Definition
Actual Cost
The cost computed from actually-recorded transactions (as opposed to Standard Cost), for a period.
AI
Artificial Intelligence — in Cost360, used for mapping suggestions and natural-language analytics, always under human review (Section 23, Part 3).
Audit Trail
The immutable, append-only log of material actions across the platform (Section 21, Part 3).
BOM
Bill of Materials — the list of components and quantities required to produce one unit of a parent item, potentially multi-level.
Cost Bridge
A waterfall view showing how a product's cost moves from Standard to Actual through named variance segments.
Cost Centre
An organisational unit to which costs are pooled/tracked for control and allocation purposes.
Cost Driver
The measurable quantity (e.g. machine hours) used as the basis for allocating an overhead cost pool.
Cost Pool
An aggregated bucket of indirect/overhead cost awaiting allocation to cost objects.
Costing Integrity Score
A composite score (Section 13.2, Part 3) reflecting data quality, reconciliation health and exception backlog for a period/plant — formula Proposed / To Be Confirmed.
CMA
Cost & Management Accountant — the primary operating role in Cost360 (Section 4.3, Part 1).
Data Lineage
The traceable chain from a displayed figure back to its originating source file row (Section 21.2, Part 3).
FIFO
First-In-First-Out — an inventory valuation method that consumes the oldest cost layer first.
GL
General Ledger — the accounting system of record for financial postings.
MVP
Minimum Viable Product — the Phase 1 scope defined in Section 36.
MWAC
Moving Weighted Average Cost — an inventory valuation method that recomputes the average cost after every receipt.
NRV
Net Realisable Value — estimated selling price less costs to complete and sell; relevant to inventory write-down considerations (not detailed as an MVP requirement).
Overhead Allocation
The process of distributing indirect cost pools to cost objects (e.g. products) using a defined basis and driver.
Periodic WAC
Periodic Weighted Average Cost — an inventory valuation method computing a single average rate for the whole period.
Product Cost
The total cost of producing one unit of a product: material + labour + process/machine + production overheads + other direct cost + allocated overheads (BR-COST-001).
RBAC
Role-Based Access Control — the permission model governing what each role can view/do (Section 4.7, Part 1).
Reconciliation
The process of confirming Cost360's computed figures tie out to source financial/operational records within tolerance.
SaaS
Software as a Service — Cost360's deployment/delivery model.
SKU
Stock Keeping Unit — a distinct item/product identifier.
Specific Cost
An inventory valuation method costing each issue at the exact cost of its specific lot/serial.
Standard Cost
A predetermined/budgeted cost per item/component, used as the baseline for variance analysis.
Tenant
A Cost360 customer organisation; the top-level multi-tenancy boundary (Section 16.1, Part 2, tenants table).
TB
Trial Balance — the summarised list of GL account balances for a period, used as the reconciliation source of truth.
UOM
Unit of Measure.
Variance
The difference between Actual and Standard cost, decomposed into named sub-categories (price, usage, rate, efficiency, etc.).
WAC
Weighted Average Cost — general term covering Periodic and Moving WAC methods.
WIP
Work-in-Progress — partially completed production inventory (Phase 2 costing treatment).
Yield
The ratio of actual usable output to expected output from a given input/process.
